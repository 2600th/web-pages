# Career Atlas Expansion Design Specification

**Status:** Approved direction; specification awaiting review

**Date:** 2026-08-26

**Extends:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-26-portfolio-redesign-design.md`

**Evidence sources:** public 2600th Substack posts, supplied Google Drive archive, existing public portfolio evidence, and `PRODUCT.md`

## 1. Decision

The portfolio will evolve from a recent-work showcase into a **Career Atlas**: an editorial, interactive record of systems built across games, XR, simulation, robotics, design technology, and applied AI.

The Career Atlas replaces the compressed Motion Study as the primary career-depth experience. It preserves the Living Wordmark, Three Distances, human-first identity, light/dark exposure system, static Astro foundation, and conversion goals of the approved redesign.

The site must communicate one coherent trajectory rather than a list of unrelated technologies:

> Pranshul has spent fifteen years turning emerging technology into usable systems, from games and immersive simulations to design platforms and production AI.

## 2. Why the Expansion Is Necessary

The current release disproportionately represents recent browser experiments and compresses substantial public career evidence into a few timeline labels. It does not adequately show:

- early game-programming work including The Brutal Spy, Alphaman, and Merkur Magie;
- founding and leading GreyKernel;
- IRA VR 360, IRA VR Metaverse, Machine Hunter, and MysticMojo;
- enterprise, industrial, medical, defence, and collaborative immersive systems;
- the Humanoid Robot Control System patent;
- HomeLane SpaceCraft Pro and the transition into design technology;
- early public thinking about AI-native game development;
- the through-line from real-time systems to current applied-AI products.

This omission weakens both inbound conversion and search authority because the visitor sees experiments without the career context that makes them credible.

## 3. Experience Architecture

### 3.1 First viewport: Operator x Inventor

The Living Wordmark remains the first-view identity. Supporting copy becomes more specific:

- primary identity: product and technology operator;
- distinctive advantage: long experience building real-time, spatial, and AI systems;
- current value: helping teams turn emerging technology into products, platforms, and operating capability.

Primary action: **Discuss an opportunity**.

Secondary action: **Explore the Career Atlas**.

The hero does not enumerate employers, clients, or technologies. Its job is orientation, intrigue, and action.

### 3.2 Selected systems: four practice pillars

Three Distances will feature four compound practice pillars instead of over-indexing on current repositories:

1. **Production AI** — Blocks and INCO-AI.
2. **Design Technology** — HomeLane SpaceCraft Pro and publicly supportable system context.
3. **Immersive Systems** — GreyKernel products, training, simulation, and robotics.
4. **Browser-native Lab** — Kinema, Web Ocean 3D, Safed Sagar, and related experiments.

Each pillar has `OUT`, `NEAR`, and `INSIDE` material. Individual projects remain accessible as crawlable pages.

### 3.3 Career Atlas

The Atlas is the central differentiating interaction. It combines three eras with six domains without becoming a resume timeline.

#### Eras

1. **Programmer and game maker — 2011–2015**
2. **Founder and immersive-systems builder — 2015–2021**
3. **Design-tech and AI operator — 2021–present**

#### Domains

- Games
- XR and spatial computing
- Training and simulation
- Robotics
- Design technology
- Applied AI

#### Interaction

- The default view is a legible constellation or field of evidence nodes, arranged by era and domain.
- Selecting a node changes one large media plane, factual title, date, role, concise significance, and evidence links.
- Neighboring nodes reveal relationships across time, such as game engines leading to simulation platforms or spatial-product work leading to applied AI.
- A domain filter and era filter use native buttons and linkable URL state.
- Previous/next controls, an ordered-list fallback, and complete keyboard operation are mandatory.
- Mobile uses a vertically stacked era index with a pinned or inline media stage; it does not reproduce a tiny desktop constellation.
- Reduced-motion mode uses direct state replacement with no inertial traversal.

The interaction must answer: **What was built, why did it matter, and how did it lead to the next kind of work?**

## 5. Media Editorial Plan

### 5.1 Selection principles

Use media to prove range and evolution, not to fill every record.

- Prefer motion or imagery that immediately communicates a shipped experience or functioning system.
- Prefer public promotional material over internal decks and screenshots.
- Prefer one decisive visual per system over galleries of similar frames.
- Do not present concept art, generative imagery, or reconstructed scenes as shipped work.
- Generated media may be used only for clearly decorative transitions, diagrams, textures, or social artwork.
- Every media item records source, ownership/permission status, subject, edit history, alt text, and allowed publication context.

### 5.2 Initial source shortlist

The first optimization pass should inspect and rank:

- `IRA VR` public promotional video
- `Machine Hunter` mixed-reality video and stills
- `Oye Tippa Run` video and WebP stills
- `AR Coloring / Celeste AR` video and WebP stills
- `ArchViz Interactive` video
- `Virtual Theatre / gesture` video
- `The Math Company / Virtual Supermarket` stills
- `Kave` collaboration screenshots
- `M777`, `T90`, `Tunguska`, and related simulation stills, only where safe and publicly supportable
- `GreyKernel Promo` or `GK_Demoreel`, sampled rather than imported in full
- Humanoid robot video, sampled only if it contains uniquely valuable and publishable footage
- Patent grant page or a purpose-made patent diagram derived from public patent facts

Large source videos between roughly 190 MB and 1.4 GB should not be copied into the repository by default. Inspect them remotely or download temporarily, then retain only selected excerpts and poster frames.

### 5.3 Delivery formats and budgets

#### Images

- Master crop retained outside the public build when practical.
- Responsive AVIF and WebP outputs.
- Explicit dimensions and stable aspect ratios.
- Typical wide output widths: 640, 960, 1440, and 1920 pixels where source quality supports them.
- Aim for 80–220 kB per displayed image; allow larger hero media only when visually justified.

#### Video

- Muted, inline, `playsinline`, non-autoplay by default.
- MP4/H.264 baseline plus WebM where its size benefit is material.
- Maximum normal delivery resolution: 1280x720.
- Short excerpts, generally 4–8 seconds; avoid long showreels on page load.
- Typical target: 0.8–2 MB per clip.
- Poster image always present.
- Captions or adjacent descriptive transcript for meaningful spoken or instructional material.

#### Optional career reel

One 10–15 second silent editorial montage may connect five or six eras. It must:

- use only approved source media;
- have no autoplay audio;
- respect reduced motion;
- remain lazy-loaded below the first viewport;
- stay under approximately 4 MB across its primary delivery format;
- never replace the semantic Atlas or individual evidence.

### 5.4 Processing tool choice

Use FFmpeg/FFprobe for inspection, transcoding, poster extraction, scene sampling, and montage assembly. Use Sharp or Astro's image pipeline for raster optimization. A dedicated video-generation skill is unnecessary unless selected footage requires generative reconstruction or a more complex editorial sequence. Installing a third-party skill solely for transcoding would add risk without improving the result.

## 6. Public-Safety and Confidentiality Rules

The supplied Drive archive is an evidence source, not blanket permission to publish every file.

Exclude:

- personal addresses, personal phone numbers, and private email addresses;
- participant names or data shown inside training software;
- internal architecture diagrams, network details, credentials, or unpublished workflows;
- confidential client metrics, commercial terms, or unannounced products;
- sensitive defence, medical, industrial, or operational detail;
- source imagery whose rights or public status cannot be reasonably established.

Public Substack statements may support high-level text claims. Drive media may support those claims only after a visual safety review. Defence and industrial projects should default to grouped descriptions such as **Defence and industrial simulation systems**. Specific platforms or clients may be named only when already public and contextually appropriate.

Every candidate media item receives one status:

- `public-approved`
- `public-corroborated`
- `approval-enhanced`
- `internal-reference-only`
- `excluded`

Only the first three may enter the public build, and `approval-enhanced` requires explicit user confirmation before deployment.

## 7. Visual and Interaction Direction

### Light mode: human record

- Mineral editorial ground.
- Warm, tactile media and precise black typography.
- Atlas connections read like annotations, pencil routes, and exhibition labels.
- More biography, authorship, and reflection is visible.

### Dark mode: systems view

- Optical-black ground with warm-white type and cobalt state signals.
- The same Atlas reveals system relationships, domains, dates, and technical captions.
- Transitions behave like changing an exposure or inspecting another layer, not changing to a separate website.

### Playful behavior

- Atlas nodes subtly influence the Living Wordmark's typographic axes.
- Selecting related nodes exposes a restrained connection path.
- Media planes use authored cuts and optical reveals rather than generic card expansion.
- Interface sound remains optional, off by default, and limited to short detents.
- No cursor replacement, scroll hijacking, persistent WebGL scene, or autoplay soundtrack.

## 8. Content and Data Model Changes

Add or extend typed content fields:

- `era`
- `domains`
- `recordType`: `feature`, `case`, or `evidence-note`
- `careerOrder`
- `relationships`
- `evidenceStatus`
- `mediaProvenance`
- `publicClaims`
- `engagementPath`
- `sourceOriginalPublication`

Atlas data must be rendered from the same content records as `/work/`, preventing a visual narrative from drifting away from crawlable page content.

## 9. SEO and Entity Strategy

The expansion should create topical authority around a real career, not keyword-targeted landing-page sprawl.

- Expand the global `Person` entity with only verifiable affiliations, credentials, authored work, patent information, and same-as profiles.
- Add a crawlable `/career/` or Atlas permalink if the homepage interaction becomes too dense for durable linking.
- Use `CreativeWork`, `SoftwareApplication`, `VideoObject`, `Article`, and patent-related visible citations only where appropriate and supported.
- Give every media asset descriptive filenames, captions, alt text, and width/height.
- Preserve original Substack canonical links when a local page is an excerpt; use a new local canonical only when the content is materially rewritten and expanded.
- Create internal links between systems, domains, notes, and relevant engagement paths.
- Use dates and “last reviewed” metadata where it helps distinguish historical work from current availability.

No fabricated FAQs, testimonials, outcome metrics, or schema-only claims are permitted.

## 10. Performance and Accessibility Budgets

The existing budgets remain binding:

- p75 LCP below 2.5 seconds;
- p75 INP below 200 milliseconds;
- CLS below 0.1;
- first-view JavaScript target below 90 kB compressed;
- first-view media below 1.2 MB mobile and 2 MB desktop.

Additional Atlas requirements:

- no video in the critical rendering path;
- offscreen media fetched only near interaction or viewport entry;
- no continuous animation when the Atlas is offscreen or the tab is hidden;
- selected-node changes announced accessibly without creating excessive live-region output;
- complete ordered-list and link fallback without JavaScript;
- keyboard, touch, coarse-pointer, 200% zoom, reduced-motion, forced-colors, and no-JavaScript tests.

## 11. Delivery Sequence

1. Create a structured evidence ledger with claim and media provenance.
2. Download only the shortlisted source assets into a non-public working area.
3. Inspect representative frames and remove unsafe or low-quality candidates.
4. Produce optimized image/video derivatives and a documented manifest.
5. Extend the content model and add factual career records.
6. Build the static Atlas fallback first.
7. Add Atlas interaction and theme-layer behavior.
8. Rebalance homepage selected systems and conversion paths.
9. Add the expanded work index, career narrative, and Substack-derived notes.
10. Verify accessibility, performance, metadata, structured data, and browser behavior.
11. Keep all `approval-enhanced` media out of deployment until explicitly approved.

## 12. Acceptance Criteria

A first-time qualified visitor should be able to:

- identify Pranshul's current operator/AI proposition in the first viewport;
- understand the transition from games and immersive systems to design technology and AI;
- discover at least twelve factual career moments without reading a resume dump;
- distinguish shipped work, experiments, authored ideas, and grouped confidential work;
- inspect several strong media-backed cases without downloading large media bundles;
- reach an engagement path relevant to their opportunity;
- navigate the complete experience using keyboard, touch, reduced motion, or JavaScript-disabled HTML.

The release is not complete if the Atlas is visually impressive but omits the semantic archive, media provenance, privacy review, or conversion paths.

## 13. Specification Self-Review

- The expansion preserves the approved identity and does not restart the design from scratch.
- The Career Atlas adds depth without turning the homepage into a resume.
- Media selection is evidence-led and performance-bounded.
- Public claims and Drive media are separated by an explicit publication-status model.
- Historical work strengthens, rather than competes with, the current operator proposition.
- No private metric, confidential architecture, or unpublished client claim is required for the experience to work.
- The site remains static, GitHub Pages-compatible, accessible, and progressively enhanced.
