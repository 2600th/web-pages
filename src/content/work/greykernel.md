## Building a company across products and client systems

I co-founded GreyKernel in 2015 and led technology for six years. We worked across immersive learning, enterprise XR, games, simulation, robotics, and physical training systems. The visible output changed from project to project, but the underlying work was consistent: understand the real task, choose the right form of interaction, and build the systems around the experience that made it usable.

As CTO, I worked across product definition, systems architecture, technical delivery, and the decisions needed to connect software with content, production teams, and specialist hardware. The role was not limited to writing code, and it did not remove me from implementation. It required moving between the prototype, the delivery plan, and the team responsible for taking it into use.

## The less visible half of an immersive product

The headset or game screen was usually the part people remembered. The harder product work often sat around it. IRA VR needed teacher controls and assessment. Training systems needed instructor setup and evaluation. Location experiences needed production workflows and a clear navigation model. Hardware-based simulation needed sensor input to agree with what the software displayed.

That changed how I thought about immersive technology. Presence was useful only when it supported the task. A realistic environment could still fail if the instructor could not run the session, the participant did not understand the next action, or the system could not recover when a device lost tracking.

## My role

I led product and technical work while coordinating with engineers, artists, production teams, and domain specialists. The balance changed with the project. Some needed a product direction and architecture. Others needed a prototype that could answer a specific interaction question. Client systems also required a practical delivery plan that the team could support after the demonstration.

GreyKernel taught me that a founder’s job is partly to keep those layers connected. The technical approach has to fit the user’s task, the team’s capabilities, and the conditions under which the system will run.

## What carried forward

The same questions later appeared in design software and applied AI. What does the user need to control? Which decisions must remain stable? What happens after the visible interaction? What does the next person or system need to receive?

GreyKernel is where I learned to treat the experience, the operating workflow, and the delivery system as one product problem.
